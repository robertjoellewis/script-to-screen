## Core Framing

**Central question:** Why is predicting audience response so hard — and what does science actually tell us about how entertainment works?

**ROI hook (brief):** Studios, streamers, and advertisers sink enormous capital into content and still can't reliably predict what lands. The reason ROI is hard to predict isn't insufficient data — it's that nobody has the right theoretical framework for knowing *what to measure*. One sentence, then into the argument.

## Section Notes

### 1. The ROI Problem (3 min)
- Studios, streamers, advertisers all face the same core challenge: enormous capital investment in content, unreliable prediction of what captures attention
- Current toolkit: gut instinct, focus groups, behavioral metrics (clicks, completions, views) — none grounded in validated psychological theory
- One sentence pivot: the problem isn't the data, it's the framework
- **Film students:** the scripts you write will be evaluated on ROI logic. **Ad students:** your campaigns live or die by it.

### 2. What Entertainment Science Knows — and Doesn't (7 min)
- The field is 40+ years old and legitimately fragmented — researchers still debate the definition of "entertainment"
- Two main traditions:
  - **Character-based (Zillmann):** we care about characters, moral judgment drives engagement → strong empirical record, but can't explain character-less entertainment or rewatching
  - **Play/predictive-mind (Vorderer, Fisher et al.):** entertainment as cognitive play, model-updating → broad coverage, but no structure for predicting *when* entertainment harms audiences
- The suspense paradox: if you watch out of concern for outcomes, why do 92% of U.S. audiences rewatch content they've already seen?
- Neither tradition asks the producer's question

### 3. A Different Starting Question (5 min)
- Every prior theory asks: what does entertainment do *to* audiences?
- The prior question: why does entertainment exist *at all*?
- Entertainment is made by people with goals — profit, influence, status, brand awareness. Audience benefit is a means, not the end.
- Entertainment as **deliberately iterated technology** (Csikszentmihalyi's systems view): not individual genius, but collaborative iteration through trial-and-error, with gatekeepers selecting what survives
- Screenwriters, directors, studio executives, talent agencies — the field that selects which tropes, formulas, and techniques survive

### 4. Superstimuli: What Entertainment Actually Is (10 min)
- Tinbergen (1953): birds preferred fake eggs with more exaggerated spots over their own real eggs — "supernormal stimuli"
- In humans: the same exploitation of evolved sensory preferences, via cultural-technological iteration rather than biological evolution, and much faster
- Dubourg et al. (2024): 73 categories of cognitive mechanisms across 6 evolutionary domains
  - SELF, THREATS, COOPERATORS, COMPETITORS, MATES, KIN
- Entertainment tropes ARE superstimuli:
  - Animated characters getting cuter over decades → cuteness/kin-detection superstimulus
  - Monsters getting scarier → predator/threat-detection superstimulus
  - Bodies more exaggerated → mate-selection superstimulus
  - Editing faster → attentional orienting
  - Moral conflicts more extreme → evolved cooperation mechanisms
- Producers don't consciously design superstimuli — they iterate through trial and error, amplifying whatever captures attention. The superstimuli result is emergent.
- **For film students:** your genre conventions, story formulas, and character archetypes are the accumulated residue of this evolutionary process

### 5. Why It Works: The Predictive Mind (5 min)
- Classic attention model: brain passively filters incoming stimuli
- Predictive processing (Friston, Clark): brain continuously generates probabilistic models and updates them via prediction error
- Attention = precision-weighted inference about which signals are most informative
- Superstimuli "capture" attention by triggering high-magnitude prediction errors in evolved systems
- This is the mechanism Fisher et al. (2024) identified — the current framework adds the producer-side and domain structure

### 6. What This Predicts: Cultivation Effects + Content Evolution (8 min)
- Two sides of the same feedback loop:
  1. Evolved mechanisms create attentional priorities
  2. Producers discover through trial-and-error which stimuli resonate, amplify them
  3. Audiences habituate → producers exaggerate further
  4. Repeated exposure to systematically exaggerated distributions shifts Bayesian priors
  5. Domain-specific cultivation effects result
- Table 1 examples (pick 4-5):
  - Skill acquisition → training montages → "montage effect" (underestimate real-world mastery time) **[novel prediction]**
  - Threat superstimuli → scarier content → mean world syndrome (overestimate crime rates) **[replication from first principles]**
  - Friendship superstimuli → idealized friend groups → real friendship dissatisfaction **[novel prediction]**
  - Mate superstimuli → idealized bodies/romances → body dissatisfaction, unrealistic partner standards **[replication]**
  - Warmth superstimuli → pronounced kindness → overestimate stranger prosociality **[novel, counterintuitive]**
- Key differentiator: Gerbner's cultivation theory discovered these effects inductively. This framework *predicts* them deductively from first principles — and generates novel ones Gerbner couldn't reach.

### 7. The Suspense Paradox Resolved (4 min)
- Why do we rewatch if we know the ending?
- Hierarchical Bayesian processing: the predictive mind operates at multiple levels simultaneously
  - **High-level predictions:** broad narrative structures ("hero wins," "romance succeeds") → familiarity reduces global uncertainty → positive affect
  - **Low-level predictions:** moment-to-moment events → specific scares, dialogue delivery, visual details → still generate prediction errors
- Familiarity at high levels *enables* novelty at low levels
- Producers engineer this: reliable genre conventions (high-level) + amplified specific stimuli (low-level)
- Analogy: the honest placebo effect — higher cognition knows it's simulated; lower processing systems respond genuinely anyway
- **For film students:** this is why genre matters. The formula is not laziness — it's a cognitive service to the audience.

### 8. Implications + Open Questions (3 min)
- A unified taxonomy: character research, cultivation research, body-image research are not independent silos — they're systematic explorations of different superstimuli domains
- Scope expansion: explains character-less entertainment (music, ASMR, landscapes, game mechanics) via non-moral superstimuli domains
- AI + measurement: once you have this taxonomy, you can use AI productively — constrained by the 73-mechanism checklist rather than reaching for proxies
- Tying back to ROI: framework tells you what to measure; psychometrics tells you how to measure it validly; AI scales the measurement
- Open questions: addiction and maladaptive use, cross-cultural variation, domain interactions, empirical testing of novel predictions
