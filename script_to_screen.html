The Industry Problem

Enormous Investment.
Unreliable Prediction.

Hollywood, streaming, games, and advertising spend hundreds of billions a year. None of them can reliably predict what will capture attention.

The toolkit: gut instinct, focus groups, watch-time metrics.

Not a data problem. There has never been more audience data. It's a framework problem — nobody knows what to measure.

02 / 16
Hollywood, streaming platforms, the video game industry, and advertisers collectively invest hundreds of billions annually creating content — and still cannot reliably predict what will capture audience attention. The current toolkit is gut instinct, focus groups, and behavioral metrics: clicks, completions, watch time. Screenwriting vocabulary and trends dominate the discussion. The twist is that this is not a data problem. There is more audience data now than at any point in history. It is a framework problem — nobody has the right theoretical structure for knowing what to measure in the first place.

40+ Years of Research

Entertainment Science:
A Fragmented Field

The Character Tradition
Entertainment works through concern for characters. Moral judgment drives engagement. Forty years of strong empirical support.

Zillmann, 1983

LIMITATION

Can't explain entertainment without characters — music, landscapes, game mechanics, ASMR.

The Play Tradition
Entertainment is cognitive play — leisure-time rehearsal that refines internal models of the world.

Vorderer; Fisher et al., 2024

LIMITATION

Finds the right mechanism, but can't predict when entertainment helps audiences or harms them.

03 / 16
Character tradition (Zillmann, 1983): entertainment works through audience concern for characters; moral judgment drives engagement. Disposition theory, excitation-transfer theory. Strong empirical record built over 40 years. Its limitation: it cannot explain character-less entertainment — music, landscapes, game mechanics, ASMR — and cannot resolve the suspense paradox. Play tradition (Vorderer; Fisher et al., 2024): entertainment is cognitive play, leisure-time rehearsal that refines internal models. Fisher et al. name "model-updating" as entertainment's core biological function. Its limitation: it correctly identifies the Bayesian mechanism but provides no structure for predicting when entertainment will harm audiences versus help them.

Neither Tradition Resolves These

Four Open Puzzles

THE REWATCH PARADOX

If attention needs novelty, why do 92% of audiences rewatch — and enjoy it just as much?

THE MEASUREMENT PROBLEM

Why code violence and not editing pace? No principled basis for choosing what to measure.

THE INTENSIFICATION PUZZLE

Monsters get scarier. Characters get cuter. Editing gets faster. Why, in predictable directions?

THE HARM PREDICTION GAP

Which audiences are harmed, in which domains, under what conditions? Harm is treated as an exception.

04 / 16
Rewatch paradox: if attention depends on novelty and uncertainty, why do 92% of U.S. audiences rewatch content they have already seen — and enjoy it just as much? Measurement problem: neither framework tells you what to measure in content. Why code violence rates and not editing pace? There's no principled basis for selecting which features are consequential. Intensification puzzle: monsters get scarier decade to decade, characters get cuter, editing gets faster — neither tradition explains why entertainment systematically intensifies in predictable directions. Harm prediction gap: neither framework predicts which audiences are harmed, in which domains, under what conditions. Harmful effects get treated as exceptions, not as something theory should have seen coming.

The Producer Turn

Why Does Entertainment
Exist At All?

Every prior theory asks what entertainment does to audiences. None asks why it gets made.

Producers have goals: profit, influence, status. Audience benefit is a means, not the end.

Entertainment is a deliberately iterated technology — refined by competitive trial and error over centuries.

06 / 16
Every prior theory asks: what does entertainment do to audiences? That misses something fundamental — the question of why entertainment gets made at all. Producers have goals: profit, political influence, status, brand awareness. Audience benefit is a means, not the end. Entertainment exists because it captures attention in service of something producers want. So entertainment is a deliberately iterated technological system — not a natural phenomenon, not a side effect of human creativity, but a technology built by people for a purpose. Screenwriters, directors, studios, and agencies form a field that selects which tropes, story formulas, and techniques survive. The ones that survive are the ones that capture attention.

Tinbergen, 1953

Superstimuli:
The Key Concept

Birds abandoned their own eggs to sit on fakes with bigger spots. The exaggerated stimulus beat the real one.

The claim: producers have been doing this to human audiences — by cultural iteration, far faster than evolution could manage.

Tropes and genre conventions are superstimuli.

07 / 16
Niko Tinbergen discovered that birds would abandon their own real eggs to incubate fake eggs with larger, more exaggerated spots. The supernormal stimulus — unnaturally intense — hijacked an evolved response more effectively than the natural stimulus it was designed for. The claim here: entertainment producers have been doing exactly this to human audiences, through cultural-technological iteration rather than biology, and much faster than evolution could manage. Entertainment tropes, story formulas, and genre conventions are superstimuli: systematically exaggerated stimuli that tap evolved psychological mechanisms more intensely than natural stimuli ever could. Producers iterate and amplify what works; the superstimuli result is emergent — the accumulated residue of what captured attention across centuries of competition.

Dubourg et al., 2024

Superstimuli in Action

Producers Amplify
Whatever Gets a Reaction

These aren't accidents of taste. They're the residue of decades of competitive iteration.

EVOLUTION

Characters keep getting cuter. Monsters scarier. Editing faster. None of it is coincidence.

MECHANISM

Audiences habituate, so producers exaggerate further. The ratchet only turns one way.

10 / 16
These images are not accidents of taste. They are the accumulated result of a competitive selection process across decades — each iteration slightly more exaggerated than the last. Animated characters have gotten progressively cuter since the 1930s; monsters progressively scarier; romantic leads progressively more idealized; editing progressively faster. The mechanism: as audiences habituate to one level of intensity, producers exaggerate further to recapture the same attentional response. The ratchet only goes one direction.

Why Superstimuli Work

The Predictive Brain

Attention isn't passive filtering. The brain predicts first, then attends to whatever breaks the prediction.

Predictive processing: the brain models the world and updates when reality deviates. Attention weights which errors matter.

Friston, 2010 · Clark, 2013

WHAT SUPERSTIMULI DO

Force prediction errors larger than nature ever produces. The brain can't ignore them.

THE PRODUCER INSIGHT

Producers don't know Bayesian inference. They know what gets a reaction.

Prediction error minimization
as the basis of attention

11 / 16
The classical model of attention — passive filtering of incoming stimuli — is wrong. The brain does not wait for input and then respond. Predictive processing (Friston, 2010; Clark, 2013): the brain continuously generates probabilistic models of the world and updates them when reality deviates from prediction. Attention is the brain's way of precision-weighting which prediction errors are most informative. What superstimuli do: trigger high-magnitude prediction errors in evolved sensory and motivational systems — larger errors than any naturally occurring stimulus would produce. The brain cannot ignore them. And producers don't know Bayesian inference; they just know what gets a reaction. Through trial and error they've been engineering maximal prediction errors in evolved systems for centuries.

If the brain updates its probabilistic model of the world from experience — and if entertainment systematically presents an exaggerated version of the world — then heavy consumers should develop systematically distorted beliefs. This is the mechanism behind Gerbner's cultivation theory, derived here from first principles rather than discovered inductively. Note the mix of replications and genuinely novel predictions, and especially the "rosy world" effects in the warmth and cooperation domains — differential predictions that classical cultivation theory cannot make.

Gojira, 1954 · a threat superstimulus

14 / 16
92% of U.S. audiences rewatch content they have already seen, to completion — and enjoy it. Resolution: hierarchical Bayesian processing. The predictive mind operates at multiple levels simultaneously, and familiarity at high levels enables novelty at low levels. High-level predictions cover broad narrative structure — "hero prevails," "romance succeeds." Knowing the ending creates certainty there, which reduces global uncertainty and produces positive affect. Low-level predictions cover moment-to-moment stimuli — specific scares, sound design, dialogue delivery, superstimuli activations — and those generate new prediction errors every time. So genre conventions are not laziness; they are a cognitive service to the audience. Reliable high-level structure minimizes cognitive load so low-level superstimuli can do their work.
